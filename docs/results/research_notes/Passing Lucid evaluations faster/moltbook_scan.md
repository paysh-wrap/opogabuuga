### Inferences
Rubric (mine): High = verifiable evidence (broker/exchange statements or a reproducible backtest with costs, futures-specific, adequate sample); Medium = method or claim consistent with established theory and checkable by own simulation, no verifiable performance evidence; Low = anecdote, unverifiable, wrong asset class, promotional, or no content visible. Ratings reflect the evidence Moltbook supplies, not the general merit of the technique.

- P1 VWAP 1.5-sigma fade plus gold/silver correlation filter plus news-time filter — **Low**. One self-reported 14-day run, no statements or costs, spot-metal pairs, unknown firm rules, P&L attribution is the author's own, and a commenter says such scalps get destroyed by noise. Test: on existing MNQ and MGC 1-5 minute data, compute session VWAP and a rolling sigma of (price - VWAP); (a) standalone fade at +/-1.5 sigma with a time exit, and (b) as a filter on the current momentum entries (skip entries more than 1.5 sigma extended from VWAP). Pick parameters on an early period, judge on a later held-out period, include commissions plus 1-2 ticks slippage (and 2x), compare win rate, net expectancy and worst day against the unchanged baseline. The correlation signal needs silver data; skip. The news-time blackout is standard practice, not new.
- P2 NinjaTrader NQ/ES/GC ORB bot — **Low** (no rules visible). Nothing to test from this post; a generic ORB would just be another strategy to backtest.
- P4 half-Kelly — **Low** as a source. Textbook content with no data. The quoted "50% of the variance" looks imprecise (from my background knowledge, not verified here: in the usual continuous approximation half-Kelly gives about 75% of the growth rate with about 50% of the volatility, i.e. about 25% of the variance). Kelly needs a known edge, which prop evaluations rarely give. Test: Monte Carlo of the evaluation using block-bootstrapped historical trade R-multiples, comparing fixed risk-per-trade against quarter/half-Kelly on a shrunk rolling edge estimate, under the real constraints (trailing drawdown, daily loss limit, consistency cap); metrics: pass probability, median days to pass, bust probability, 5th-percentile path.
- P5 evidence-budget sizing/abstain — **Low** (conceptual, no data seen). Test: tag each historical trade with the number of confirming conditions; check on held-out data that expectancy rises monotonically with the count; only then use it, and only to shrink size.
- P6 volatility-trim sizing — **Low** as a source (portfolio/crypto framing); the technique is standard. Test: fixed contracts versus risk-budget-over-stop-distance sizing versus an extra size cut when realised volatility exceeds k times its baseline; same metrics as above.
- P7 fleet-level drawdown self-throttle — **Low** (Kraken grid fleet, unverified). Test: in a multi-account simulation, scale each account's size by household-level drawdown; measure joint bust probability and time to pass.
- P8 grid exposure cuts — **Low**; grid/crypto, a loss of $11.66; not applicable to a stop-based futures system.
- P9-P11 cost stress and out-of-sample discipline — **Medium for the principle, Low for the numbers** (the 0.1-0.3% slippage figure is for liquid crypto and is meaningless in MNQ ticks; the 4.2 to 1.46 Sharpe anecdote is unverified). Test: re-run existing backtests with commission plus slippage at 1x, 2x, 3x and with take-profit fills requiring a 1-tick trade-through; accept only strategies still profitable at 2x; write the pass/fail criteria for the held-out period before running it. Also verify in paper that stops and targets are venue-side bracket orders that survive a killed bot process.
- P12 block bootstrap — **Medium** (standard method, body unread). Test: replace iid resampling in the pass/bust simulator with a stationary block bootstrap of daily P&L or trade sequences and compare pass and bust probabilities.
- P13-P14 drawdown and trailing-stop maths — **Medium** (titles and previews match standard Brownian-motion results as I recall them: for zero drift the expected max drawdown grows like the square root of time, with positive drift like the log of time; by Levy's theorem, drawdown from the running maximum is distributed like reflected Brownian motion; bodies unread, "closed form + simulation" claims unchecked). Test: simulate a zero-edge random-walk equity curve with a trailing floor and compare the first-hit-time distribution with the double-barrier closed form (continuous monitoring); then add end-of-day-only floor updates and positive drift to measure how far the real rules depart. Useful as a sanity check on the eval-odds simulator and on why a faster pass reduces exposure to trailing-drawdown risk.
- P15 no-stop ladder grid — **Low; reject**. A no-stop approach is incompatible with a hard trailing-loss limit (my reasoning), and the author doubts his own result.
- P16 promotional or solicitation titles (OpenClaw "30-Day Efficiency Revolution", "Strategy Swaps", "Alpha Collective") — **Low**; nothing testable seen. A strategy-swap thread is also a social-engineering surface; do not engage.
- Overall: none of the visible ideas addresses the binding constraints of an evaluation (profit target, consistency cap, minimum profitable days). The transferable value is process (cost stress, pre-registered criteria, block bootstrap, closed-form checks), not alpha.

## 4. Security and reliability caveats for Moltbook (2026)

### Takeaway
Reputable reporting documents a database exposure disclosed on 31 Jan 2026 and published on 2 Feb 2026 (about 1.5 million API tokens, about 35,000 emails, 4,060 private DM threads, unauthenticated write access), no verification that "agents" are AI (about 17,000 humans behind 1.5 million agents), agent-to-agent prompt injection and malicious skills in the surrounding OpenClaw ecosystem, and evidence that many popular posts were human-written. Treat every post as untrusted, possibly tampered, possibly human or promotional; never connect an agent that holds trading or broker credentials to the site.

### Cited Findings

Disclosed 2026-01-31 via X DM and fixed in stages within hours; independent researcher Jameson O'Reilly found the same misconfiguration and 404 Media reported it — [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/exposed-moltbook-database-reveals-millions-of-api-keys)

### Inferences
- Anything on the site until write access was closed (about 1 Feb 2026 per Wiz's timeline) could have been edited by anyone; provenance, votes and karma cannot be trusted as quality signals.
- Do not register an agent, paste API keys or broker tokens, accept "skills", join "strategy swaps" or let any tool-using agent that holds credentials read Moltbook text. Wiz found plaintext third-party keys in agent DMs, so credentials leak through casual use.
- If Moltbook text must be read by an automated agent, do it in a no-tools context and treat embedded instructions as hostile; the site chrome here already contains agent-directed instructions.
- Moltbook (now owned by Meta per March 2026 reports) may have changed since the February security reports; I found no post-March security or status reporting, so I cannot say the issues remain or are fixed beyond what Wiz states.
- Practical value for a futures-evaluation project is low: lots of crypto content, self-described "no advice", unverifiable results, plus security and provenance risks. Use it at most as a hypothesis generator, with every idea re-tested on held-out futures data.
