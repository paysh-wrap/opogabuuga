Scope and labels (as of 2026-11-22). Web research only; lucidtrading.com and tradingview.com were never fetched; nothing was posted anywhere. Reddit was blocked for the search tool, so forum coverage is thin. Every Lucid rule below comes from third-party pages and is **[3P-UNVERIFIED]**. ## 1. What pass-rate and payout statistics exist (2023-2026), and what distinguishes passers?

### Takeaway
Only a few firms publish outcome statistics (Topstep 2025: 16.8% of Combines passed vs 51.8% of individuals; Tradeify Aug 2025-Jul 2026: 17.2% vs 40.3%; Take Profit Trader 2025: 36.22%; MyFundedFutures ~20%); Lucid and Apex publish none that I could find. No firm or credible third party publishes data on what distinguishes passers (trade frequency, size, days to pass, instruments); the only measured pattern is persistence (per-individual success is 2.3x-3.1x per-attempt success), while vendor simulations point to trailing-drawdown breaches as the dominant failure mode and to risk sized against the drawdown, not the account, as the main lever.

### Cited Findings
Firm-published (self-reported, unaudited, mixed denominators):
- Topstep, Jan-Dec 2025 [FIRM]: 16.8% of all Trading Combines initiated were "successfully completed and afforded the opportunity to advance to the Funded Level"; 51.8% of individual participants who entered one or more Combines advanced at least once; 33.3% of individuals at the Funded Level received a payout; 0.71% of individuals in an Express Funded Account were called up to Live. "Funded Level" aggregates Express and Live accounts; figures blend simulated and live environments and are "not indicative of future results." — [Topstep Risk Disclosure](https://www.topstep.com/risk-disclosure)
- Tradeify, Aug 2025-Jul 2026, calculated 9 Aug 2026 (Growth/Advanced/Select) [FIRM, COI, no sample size given]: 17.2% of evaluation accounts initiated advanced to Funded Level; 40.3% of individuals advanced in at least one; median evaluations initiated per participant = 3; 28.5% of Funded-Level participants received a payout; 3.0% moved to Live Funded. The same page lists "not reported": trade frequency, contract size, failure reasons, instruments, automation metrics. — [Tradeify: Futures Prop Firm Statistics 2026](https://tradeify.co/post/futures-prop-firm-statistics-2026)
- Take Profit Trader 36.22% of Trading Tests passed in 2025 (16.86% in 2024); MyFundedFutures 19.97% of evaluation participants advanced beyond the evaluation (period not stated); Earn2Trade 8.89% of new subscriptions passed in 2025. The page says most firms publish no pass rate and lists none for Apex, Tradeify, Lucid, Alpha Futures or Bulenox. — [FundedSim](https://fundedsim.com/en/guides/prop-firm-pass-rates) (secondary relay of firm disclosures)
- "No major futures prop firm publicly discloses its verified evaluation pass rate"; the oft-quoted 5-15% (10% most cited) per-attempt figure is triangulated from community self-reports and a self-described "speculative" payout reverse-engineering with no primary dataset; "pass rate by attempt" (1st 5-10%, 2nd 15-25%, 3rd 20-30%) is self-reported community data with acknowledged selection bias. Apex self-reports $882.09M+ total payouts since 2022; Tradeify $250M+ paid to 80,000+ funded traders since June 2024 (payout scale, not pass rates). Page is affiliate-driven and promotes Apex. — [TraderPayout](https://traderpayout.com/prop-firms/prop-firm-pass-rate) [COI, [MARKETING]-adjacent]
- Per-attempt vs per-person: Topstep's 16.8% vs 51.8% differ because of resets and repeat purchases (fail three, pass the fourth = one pass in four attempts but one success per person); multiplying Topstep's 51.8% x 33.3% gives roughly 17 of 100 people starting a Combine seeing a payout, with the caveat that populations do not match exactly. — [FundedSim](https://fundedsim.com/en/guides/prop-firm-pass-rates)

What distinguishes passers (evidence available):
- No firm data on passers' trade frequency, size, instruments or days-to-pass: Tradeify lists them as not reported ([Tradeify](https://tradeify.co/post/futures-prop-firm-statistics-2026)); TraderPayout offers no measured data on any factor and says psychology-causes-most-failures style claims are unsourced ([TraderPayout](https://traderpayout.com/prop-firms/prop-firm-pass-rate)).
- LuxAlgo Monte Carlo (rules verified 2026-01-15; 10,000 paths per cell; i.i.d. trades) [SIM, COI affiliate-disclosed]. Modeled pass % per attempt for developing / consistent / proven-edge profiles (developing = 45% win rate, 1.4R winners, 4 trades/day, 1% risk; consistent = 48%, 1.6R, 4/day, 0.75%; proven = 52%, 1.8R, 3/day, 0.5%): Topstep 50K 38.1 / 71.0 / 95.6; Apex 50K Full Eval 45.6 / 76.8 / 97.7; MFFU Rapid EOD 50K 29.2 / 68.3 / 95.3; TPT 50K 44.3 / 72.9 / 95.6; Tradeify Select 50K 33.5 / 69.7 / 95.4. Page states the trailing drawdown, not the profit target, is the leading cause of failure; Lucid is a tracked firm but has no simulated pass rate on the page. — [LuxAlgo Futures Prop Firm Simulator](https://www.luxalgo.com/prop-firms/futures/)
- Vendor simulations [SIM, page itself says results are ceilings and optimistic for Apex]: at zero expected value per trade a $50K Apex pass rate is 17-26%; at 46% win rate / 1.2 payoff it is 30.1% with 3.3 evaluations per funded account; Topstep $50K failure mix at 2 x $150 risk/day = 67% drawdown, 33% ran out of time; at 4 x $150 = 94% / 6%; at 4 x $300+ >99.9% drawdown; Apex sim: 97% of failures were trailing-drawdown breaches; median 39 days to pass ($50K Apex, "realistic profile"), slowest tenth 71+ days. — [PropFirmPineScripts](https://propfirmpinescripts.com/tools/prop-firm-evaluation-statistics.html) [COI vendor]
- Same page, size vs drawdown (46% win rate, 1.2 payoff): with risk scaled to the account, pass rate is ~30% at every size ($25K 29.8%, $50K 30.1%, $100K 30.4%, $150K 30.1%); with flat $150 risk it collapses on bigger accounts ($25K 32.0%, $50K 29.9%, $100K 7.6%, $150K 0.7%). The page's own internal strategy tests (no methodology): ES 32.2%, MGC 48.0% (385 trades, 10,000 sims) on Apex, MNQ on $250K 0% within a month. — [PropFirmPineScripts](https://propfirmpinescripts.com/tools/prop-firm-evaluation-statistics.html)
- Closed-form baseline [CALC]: with no edge, fixed-size daily wins/losses and a fixed floor, P(target before floor) = drawdown / (drawdown + target): $2,000 vs $3,000 = ~40%; Topstep 150K ~33%; a trailing floor lowers this, a real edge raises it; smaller size only helps when there is an edge (55% green-day trader on 50K: $500 days ~64% pass, $250 days ~81%, at the cost of more days). — [FundedSim](https://fundedsim.com/en/guides/prop-firm-pass-rates)
- Academic context: Tradeify's page cites SSRN "Day Trading for a Living?" for a 97% day-trader-loss figure (Brazilian equity-index futures day traders, 2013-2015) - not a prop-firm pass rate. — [Tradeify](https://tradeify.co/post/futures-prop-firm-statistics-2026)

### Inferences
- Firm "pass rates" mostly measure persistence, not first-attempt skill: if attempts were i.i.d. at p = 16.8%, a 51.8% individual success rate implies ~4.0 attempts per participant (1 - 0.832^k = 0.518); Tradeify's 40.3% at 17.2% implies ~2.7, consistent with its stated median of 3. (My arithmetic; attempts are not i.i.d., so directional only.) "Passers" in firm data are therefore largely repeat buyers.
- The firm-published per-attempt range (16.8%-36.2%) is far below LuxAlgo's "consistent trader" band (68.3%-76.8% at 50K), which is itself an upper bound (i.i.d., no tilt). (internal context) The requester's sim pass rates (69% at 50K, 77% at 150K) sit inside that band: plausible for a rule-following bot, but they should be haircut for fills, intra-trade excursions and i.i.d. assumptions (LuxAlgo's README says these overstate pass odds at tight loss limits; see section 2).
- The structural variable is target:drawdown. Using third-party-reported Lucid targets/MLLs (section 4) in the zero-edge formula gives 25K: 1,000/2,250 = 44.4%; 50K: 2,000/5,000 = 40.0%; 100K: 3,000/9,000 = 33.3%; 150K: 4,500/13,500 = 33.3% (fixed-floor upper bound; the EOD trail lowers it). The 100K/150K tiers carry a 2.0 target:MLL ratio vs 1.5 at 50K and 1.25 at 25K.
- Modeled failure mix (drawdown >> time) says reliability is about surviving the floor; speed is about risk per unit of drawdown; the two trade off (see sections 2, 3, 6).

### Gaps
- Lucid and Apex publish no pass-rate or payout-rate statistics that I found; Alpha Futures none found. A claim that "only 18% of funded Lucid traders ever receive payouts", a 5-10% LucidFlex pass estimate and a 76% figure from user-submitted PropTraderz reports surfaced in search summaries only; none could be attributed to a page I read, so treat as unverified.
- No firm publishes median days-to-pass, trades/day, size-vs-drawdown or instrument breakdowns for passers. The only time-to-pass figure is the vendor sim above.
- MyFundedFutures "39.2% of participants reaching the Funded Stage earned a payout" and the FPFX Technology / Finance Magnates (Sept 2024) cross-firm figures (~14% of traders passed, ~7% paid; mostly forex-heavy) appeared in search summaries but were not verified at page level.
- The rswebsols "2026 Prop Firm Transparency Report" (https://www.rswebsols.com/news/2026-prop-firm-transparency-report-shows-wide-gap-between-per-attempt-and-per-trader-pass-rates/) returned HTTP 403; its figures were not read. A claim that Take Profit Trader's 2025 jump (16.86% to 36.22%) coincided with a cut in minimum Test days from five to three in Aug 2026 appeared only in review-site search summaries (not verified; a rule change could explain a pass-rate change).

## 2. How do experienced evaluation traders handle consistency rules, daily profit caps, EOD vs intraday trailing, and the lock level - and which tactics have evidence rather than anecdote?

### Takeaway
The only primary-source design information is Topstep's: a consistency rule raises the profit you must reach rather than failing you, and Lucid's rule is described the same way by third parties (holds the upgrade/payout until the ratio is met). Practitioner tactics (cap each day near target x ratio, ration good days, delay withdrawals) are arithmetic-consistent but supported only by competitor/affiliate blog opinion and a few forum anecdotes; the only quantified claims on EOD vs intraday trailing are vendor-modeled (intraday trailing cuts pass odds by roughly a third vs a static floor) with disclosed optimism, and I found no outcome data on daily-profit-cap strategies.

### Cited Findings
Rule design (what consistency rules actually do):
- Topstep help center (primary, read Nov 2026): Combine consistency target = 55%; violating it does not fail you, it raises the target ("need to earn more to pass"). Example: a $1,800 best day makes the adjusted target ~$3,273 ($1,800 / 0.55); if the next day earns $2,200 the best day becomes $2,200 and the target $4,000; losses do not reset your best day; Combine can be passed in as few as 2 days. The article is internally inconsistent on the base (best day vs profit target vs total profit). — [Consistency at Topstep](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/8284208-consistency-at-topstep)
- Lucid [3P-UNVERIFIED] metric = "largest profitable trading day divided by profit for the relevant evaluation or payout cycle"; exceeding it "holds up the upgrade or payout until the requirement is met" (not described as a breach); minimum profitable days implied by the math: 40% or 35% = at least 3, 20% = at least 5. Page updated Nov 6, 2026. — [ProPropTradingVibes](https://proptradingvibes.com/blog/lucid-trading-consistency-rules)
- Cross-firm design (as of the page date): consistency 55% at Topstep, 50% at Take Profit Trader, 40% at LucidFlex, none at Alpha Futures Zero or MyFundedFutures Builder. — [FundedSim](https://fundedsim.com/en/guides/prop-firm-pass-rates). Tradeify: Growth evaluation no consistency (can pass in 1 day), Select evaluation 40% consistency (min 3 days). — [Tradeify](https://tradeify.co/post/futures-prop-firm-statistics-2026)

Practitioner/blog tactics (all [OPINION] unless noted):
- Set a daily ceiling before trading = target x ratio (e.g., $1,200 on a 50K at 40%); anything above only extends the evaluation. Size to the max loss, not the target (e.g., risk $400/day against $2,000 room survives four bad days). Near the finish, slow down rather than speed up. The DLL "books a loss rather than avoiding one" but can stop a tilt day. — [Phidias: How to pass Lucid](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/how-to-pass-lucid) [COI: competing prop firm; Lucid figures read Oct 27, 2026]
- Workarounds traders reportedly use against the Lucid Pro funded 40% rule: ration good days by trading smaller, and delay withdrawals until the ratio is met; the page presents these as burdensome bookkeeping, not as recommendations. — [Phidias: LucidPro review](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/lucidpro-review) [COI]
- Affiliate-style explainers (snippets only; pages not fetched) describe the same mechanics - a daily stop at a fraction of target, minimum profitable days = 1/ratio - and conflict with each other on Apex's rule (one table lists a 30% evaluation cap, another no evaluation rule and a 50% payout cap): [passproptradingfirms.com](https://www.passproptradingfirms.com/rules/consistency-rule/), [propfirmmap.com](https://propfirmmap.com/blog/prop-firm-consistency-rules-explained-strategies-pass-2026), [hnlgrowth.com](https://hnlgrowth.com/blog/prop-firm-consistency-rule), [backtrex.com](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/prop-firm-consistency-rule-30-percent-explained).
- Topstep how-to-pass guides (snippets only; not fetched) advise 1-3 trades/day, warn that sizing to 3-4 contracts to hit the target faster causes most Combine failures, and warn against chasing $1,000 days: [tradecovex](https://tradecovex.com/guides/pass-topstep-combine), [h2tfunding](https://h2tfunding.com/how-to-pass-topstep-combine/), [propjournal](https://propjournal.net/guides/how-to-pass-topstep) [OPINION, affiliate-style].
- Forum [ANECDOTE], Elite Trader, Jan 11-12, 2025 (a different firm, TradeFundrr): "a $1,250 target means making $250 five times", most passers finish above target "possibly up to around $2,500"; a poster relayed that a Topstep representative said the trailing limit is calculated on end-of-day PnL, not unrealized. — [Elite Trader thread](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/trading-with-futures-prop-firms-2025.382648/page-3)

EOD vs intraday trailing and the lock (quantified claims):
- Intraday trailing drawdown "routinely cuts pass probability by a third" vs static at the same limit; drawdown type is the most consequential rule choice [SIM, vendor claim, no table]. Documented limits: intra-trade excursions are not modeled (equity checked at trade close, so trailing-drawdown risk is understated and real odds "somewhat worse"); attempts are i.i.d. and i.i.d. math overstates pass probability for tight loss limits; consistency caps raise the effective target and can push extra days into trailing-floor breaches (no numbers); daily profit caps "not covered specifically." — [LuxAlgo prop-firm-sim (MIT)](https://github.com/LuxAlgo/prop-firm-sim); same page: four trailing variants (static, end-of-day, intraday-unrealized, trailing-that-locks), no per-type pass rates — [LuxAlgo Futures Simulator](https://www.luxalgo.com/prop-firms/futures/)
- Lucid EOD floor and lock [3P-UNVERIFIED]: the floor rises with each record EOD close until a close passes the initial trail balance (starting balance + MLL + $100: 26,100 / 52,100 / 103,100 / 154,600 for 25K/50K/100K/150K), then locks at starting balance + $100. — [DamnPropFirms](https://damnpropfirms.com/prop-firms/lucid-trading-rules-payouts/); [TradeTanto](https://tradetanto.com/learn/lucid-trading-rules-explained-every-plan-rule-and-limit); [Phidias](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/how-to-pass-lucid)

### Inferences
- Consistency arithmetic (mine): with ratio c, the total needed is max(target, best day / c). Each $1 of best-day profit above c x target raises the required total by $1/c (= $2.50 at 40%), so the other days must supply $1.50 more per extra $1 (1/c - 1). That is the mathematical case for a day stop at or just under c x target; it is supported by Topstep's published design, not by outcome data.
- Because the ratio uses total (not target) profit, a static cap at c x target is conservative once profit exceeds target: keeping B/(P_prev + B) <= c allows B up to c/(1-c) x P_prev (= 0.667 x P_prev at 40%), plus the prior-best-day check. A ratio-aware dynamic cap is therefore a distinct, untested alternative to a static 38%-of-target stop.
- Evidence ranking: (A) Topstep's primary rule text; (B) vendor Monte Carlo (disclosed optimism); (C) blog opinion; (D) forum anecdote. Nothing found measures a daily-profit-cap strategy's effect on pass rate or time.
- The EOD-vs-intraday risk that is not covered by anyone: whether an EOD-trailing MLL is tested against intraday equity touches (floor from last EOD) or against closed balance only. I found no third-party statement on Lucid either way; this is a simulator sensitivity, not a settled rule.

### Gaps
- No outcome data (observed or published sim) on daily-profit-cap, taper, drawdown-brake or cushion-sizing tactics; no peer-reviewed prop-challenge optimal-risk study found. The closest general paper in the search results (not read, not prop-specific): [Drawdown Risk Beyond Brownian Motion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00127).
- Reddit could not be searched; futures.io content was not retrieved; practitioner coverage is one 2025 Elite Trader page plus blogs. No credible "how I passed Lucid Pro" write-up with numbers was found.
- Whether the Lucid EOD MLL is breached on intraday touch vs closed balance: unverified (Lucid's own text was not read by rule).

## 3. Economics of account-size choice and resets: fees vs pass odds vs payout rules; smaller account repeatedly or larger once?

### Takeaway
No published real-data comparison exists. The evidence is arithmetic and vendor simulation: pass odds are roughly size-invariant when risk scales with the drawdown, but Lucid's larger tiers carry a worse target:MLL ratio; third-party Lucid prices show a one-time evaluation fee, resets at roughly 56-69% of list price, and first-payout caps that grow far slower than account size - so fees are small relative to payout caps and time-to-funded and pass probability dominate cost, while smaller sizes look favorable per fee dollar (all prices unverified, conflicting, and promo-driven).

### Cited Findings
Frameworks and simulations:
- Cost per pass is roughly price / pass odds plus activation: an Apex 50K EOD evaluation at $59 with 40% odds is ~$148 in evaluation fees plus $129 activation; at 64% odds ~$92 [CALC]. Smaller size helps only with a real edge and costs more days. — [FundedSim](https://fundedsim.com/en/guides/prop-firm-pass-rates)
- Risk sweep: pass probability favors small risk (survive the floors) while EV favors larger risk because fees are fixed and payouts scale; an `optimal-risk` command produces the curve (shown as a chart only). Scaling plans are not simulated. — [LuxAlgo prop-firm-sim](https://github.com/LuxAlgo/prop-firm-sim)
- Proportional risk gives ~30% pass at every account size; flat $150 risk gives 32.0% / 29.9% / 7.6% / 0.7% from $25K to $150K [SIM]. — [PropFirmPineScripts](https://propfirmpinescripts.com/tools/prop-firm-evaluation-statistics.html)
- Illustrative formulas [model, no observed data]: new-evaluation-only expected cost = E/p; reset path (uncapped) = E + r(1-p)/p (example p = 0.5, E = $100, r = $80 gives $200 vs $180); reset caps of 2-3 truncate this; hidden costs excluded. Apex reportedly stopped offering resets on new evaluations from Mar 1, 2026 (buy new at full price); typical reset fee quoted $50-$150 for a 100K account. — [Prop-Ledger](https://prop-ledger.org/learn/paper/account-resets-vs-new-evaluations-the-economics-of-retry-vs-walk-away)

Lucid prices, resets and payout rules [3P-UNVERIFIED]:
- Pro reset fees with DLL on (page updated Oct 27, 2026): $75 / $120 / $190 / $255 for 25K / 50K / 100K / 150K; Pro 50K with DLL off $150 (turning DLL off also turns on-demand payouts off); Flex $45 / $80 / $150 / $225; Daily (EOD) $60 / $85 / $140 / $200. Resets are paid, never automatic; breached evaluations are deleted after 30 days if not reset; a reset restores the starting balance and risk parameters. — [HomeDadPro](https://homedadpro.com/en/lucid-trading-reset/) [COI affiliate]; reset fully returns to starting balance, progress does not carry forward — [ProPropTradingVibes reset page](https://proptradingvibes.com/blog/lucid-trading-reset-cost)
- Pro evaluation prices from coupon checkouts (page dated May 25, 2026; list / coupon / reset): 25K $135 / $94.50 / $90; 50K $185 / $129.50 / $120; 100K $285 / $199.50 / $180; 150K $370 / $259 / not shown. — [Bucko](https://www.bucko.ai/learn/lucid-trading) [COI]. Conflicting price points: Pro 50K $180 list, $126 with code (rules checked Oct 31, 2026) — [PipBack](https://pipback.com/blog/lucid-trading-review); Pro 50K $172 vs Flex $115 — [Phidias LucidPro review](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/lucidpro-review). Pro 25K reset $75 (HomeDadPro, Oct 2026) vs $90 (Bucko, May 2026).
- Evaluation fees are one-time with no rebilling and no activation fee on passing; no evaluation time limit is stated in the pages read (inactivity: no trade in 30 days can lead to deletion). — [PipBack](https://pipback.com/blog/lucid-trading-review); [DamnPropFirms](https://damnpropfirms.com/prop-firms/lucid-trading-rules-payouts/); [QuantCrawler](https://quantcrawler.com/prop-firms/lucid-trading) [COI]
- DLL on is cheaper; removing it adds $21 (25K) to $64 (150K) before coupons, and the No-DLL option reportedly became permanent in Aug 2026. — [Phidias LucidPro review](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/lucidpro-review) [COI]
- Pro funded payout structure: 90/10 split; minimum request $500; per-cycle profit goal $250 / $500 / $750 / $1,000; first payout cap $1,000 / $2,000 / $2,500 / $3,000, later caps $1,500 / $2,500 / $3,000 / $3,500; buffer = initial MLL + $100; 40% consistency; five payouts then a live review pool. — [DamnPropFirms](https://damnpropfirms.com/prop-firms/lucid-trading-rules-payouts/); 3-profitable-day payout cycle for Pro vs 5 for Flex — [Phidias LucidPro review](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/lucidpro-review); 50K Pro first payout $2,000, $2,500 from payout 2 — [PipBack](https://pipback.com/blog/lucid-trading-review)
- Household limits: up to 10 active evaluations, 5 funded, 10 combined; hedging across accounts is prohibited, including through a trade copier. — [TradeTanto](https://tradetanto.com/learn/lucid-trading-rules-explained-every-plan-rule-and-limit)

### Inferences
- All arithmetic below uses unverified third-party prices and should be re-run with live checkout prices. Fee per dollar of target (Bucko list): 25K $0.108, 50K $0.062, 100K $0.048, 150K $0.041. First-payout cap per list-fee dollar: 25K 7.4, 50K 10.8, 100K 8.8, 150K 8.1 (50K is the sweet spot on this crude metric). Resets cost ~56-69% of list (e.g., 50K $120 / $185 = 65%; 150K $255 / $370 = 69%).
- Expected evaluation spend per funded account at the sim's per-start pass rates (internal context: p = 0.69 for 50K, 0.77 for 150K), list prices: 50K new-each-time $268, reset path $239; 150K new-each-time $481, reset path $446. Against first-payout caps of $2,000-$3,000, fees are minor; what matters is time-to-funded, survival after funding, and cap structure.
- The 150K first-payout cap is 1.5x the 50K's for a 3x account (2.25x MLL, 2.0 vs 1.5 target:MLL), so several smaller accounts probably earn more per fee dollar than one large account, if household caps (5 funded) and correlation allow. Copy-trading identical signals makes accounts fail together; only staggered starts decorrelate them.
- "Pass a small account repeatedly vs a large one once" cannot be answered from published data; the defensible statement is that, with one-time fees, the repeated-small route wins on fees and the target:MLL ratio and loses on payout size per account.

### Gaps
- No real-data study of size-vs-pass-rate-vs-payout; no firm publishes size-specific pass rates.
- Lucid contract limits per tier, any evaluation time limit, and reset caps were not found in any third-party page; price tables conflict and change with promotions.
- Post-funding survival and payout frequency (the most assumption-heavy EV input) have no published Lucid data.

## 5. Automation-specific evidence: are bots allowed, do they pass at different rates, and which rules (news, hold time, bots) change what is optimal?

### Takeaway
Third parties citing Lucid's support pages say automated systems and trade copiers are permitted without approval or ownership proof, with the trader responsible for software errors, but HFT, microscalping (over 50% of profit from trades held 5 seconds or less is flagged), hedging (including across accounts and firms) and exploiting system errors are prohibited. Other firms differ sharply (Apex 4.0 and Take Profit Trader ban automation; Tradeify requires proof of sole ownership; Topstep allows API automation but bans VPS). I found no credible head-to-head data on algorithmic vs discretionary pass rates - only vendor claims and anecdotes.

### Cited Findings
- Policy comparison, firm help centers as of Sep 21-22, 2026 (article updated Nov 7, 2026): Lucid - "Automated trading systems and trade copiers are permitted," trader responsible for software errors, no approval/ownership proof, HFT banned (written warning, then forfeited profit/closure, permanent ban on repeat), microscalping flagged when over 50% of profit comes from trades held 5 seconds or less, hedging across own accounts banned. Topstep - bots allowed via TopstepX/ProjectX API subject to HFT ban (banned strategies include "average hold time is seconds rather than minutes"), VPS/VPN/remote servers prohibited, API not available on Live. Apex - "No Automation or Algorithm Usage allowed" for all accounts since Apex 4.0 (Mar 1, 2026). MFFU - permits automation tailored to own settings if not exploiting sim fills. Take Profit Trader - automation not permitted (Universal Trading Policies, Mar 6, 2026). Alpha Futures - AI/bots prohibited, semi-automation only if manually placed/managed; HFT defined as over 100 trades/day; tick scalping under 10 ticks and under 2 minutes. Bulenox - user-built personal-use tools only. — [Prop-Memo: 28 Futures Prop Firms Compared](https://prop-memo.com/en/articles/futures-prop-firms-automation-2026-09)

### Inferences
- Automation's plausible advantage is behavioral (no tilt, perfect rule compliance), which is exactly what the i.i.d. simulators assume; the live-vs-sim gap for a bot is therefore execution (fills, slippage, latency), intra-trade excursions and rule misreading rather than psychology.

## 6. Tactics worth testing in the simulator (derived from sections 1-5)

### Takeaway
The best-supported levers are (i) pre-registering rule-interpretation uncertainty as sensitivities, (ii) consistency-aware profit management, (iii) risk expressed as a fraction of the drawdown with regime changes at the EOD lock, and (iv) size/account-count economics with staggered starts; evidence for each is mostly arithmetic and vendor simulation, so each needs to be judged on held-out data and compared against the already-tested daily locks, tapering, drawdown brakes and cushion-growth sizing.

### Cited Findings
- Consistency rules raise the effective target (Topstep primary design) and EOD floor locks at starting balance + $100 once the initial trail balance is reached [3P]. — [Topstep](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/8284208-consistency-at-topstep); [TradeTanto](https://tradetanto.com/learn/lucid-trading-rules-explained-every-plan-rule-and-limit)
- Pass probability favors small risk, EV favors larger; i.i.d. and no-intra-trade-excursion assumptions overstate pass odds; real R-multiples can be fed with a block bootstrap to keep autocorrelation. — [LuxAlgo prop-firm-sim](https://github.com/LuxAlgo/prop-firm-sim)
- Proportional risk makes pass rates roughly size-invariant; zero-edge odds are drawdown / (drawdown + target). — [PropFirmPineScripts](https://propfirmpinescripts.com/tools/prop-firm-evaluation-statistics.html); [FundedSim](https://fundedsim.com/en/guides/prop-firm-pass-rates)
- Lucid Pro payout caps, household limits and reset prices are as listed in sections 3-5 [3P-UNVERIFIED]. — [DamnPropFirms](https://damnpropfirms.com/prop-firms/lucid-trading-rules-payouts/); [TradeTanto](https://tradetanto.com/learn/lucid-trading-rules-explained-every-plan-rule-and-limit); [HomeDadPro](https://homedadpro.com/en/lucid-trading-reset/)

### Inferences
Candidate tests (evidence tier in brackets; metric in parentheses):
1. Rule-interpretation sensitivity grid [B/C]: evaluation consistency {none, 40%, 50%} x DLL {off, on} x MLL check {closed balance, intraday touch} x LucidScale-in-evaluation {yes, no} x Pro-25K DLL {none, $600} (pass %, median days, 10th/90th percentile days). Purpose: quantify how much of the headline pass rate and the "bigger size passes faster" result depends on unverified rules.
2. Consistency-aware dynamic day cap [A for the rule, untested as tactic]: cap_today = min over the two ratio constraints (today's profit B and prior best day vs. final total) instead of a static c x target; compare against static 38-40% x target and no cap, with the rule on and off (pass %, days, funded-payout-cycle eligibility rate).
3. Risk expressed as a fraction of the MLL with regime breaks at the EOD lock [B]: separate risk schedules before the floor locks (floor trails EOD highs) and after (cushion = profit - $100); judge on the pass-probability vs median-days frontier rather than one point.
4. Front-load vs grind under the no-consistency reading [B]: if the Pro evaluation has no ratio rule, test fewer, larger-risk early days against the current smooth schedule (a one-day-pass-style tail), tracking tail risk of floor breaches.
5. DLL on vs off [C/OPINION]: DLL is a soft breach (ends the day) per third parties; test its effect on pass %, speed and on-demand-payout eligibility, with the price delta ($21-$64) and reset delta ($30 on 50K Pro).
6. Account-size ladder and staggered multi-account portfolio [B/C]: for 25K/50K/100K/150K and for N accounts started on different days, compute expected evaluation spend per funded account (E/p and reset-path formulas), expected days to first funded, expected first-payout cap per fee dollar and per day, and number of simultaneously funded accounts, with household limits (5 funded) and correlated failures made explicit.
7. Reset vs buy-new policy [C]: reset-path cost (about 56-69% of list per third parties) vs promotion-priced new purchases, with and without a reset cap, using bootstrapped p.
8. Robustness haircut [B]: block-bootstrap real R-multiples, add intra-trade excursion (MAE) checks against the floor, slippage/fill stress and a rule-misreading scenario; report p(pass) with a haircut band instead of one number.
9. Trade-count/selection [D/OPINION]: guides advise 1-3 trades/day; test minimum-score thresholds vs trades/day for the pass-probability and speed trade-off.
10. Funded-phase carry-over [A/3P]: simulate Pro payout cycles (3 profitable days, 40%/35% ratio, caps $2,000/$2,500 on 50K, buffer MLL + $100) to see whether day-rationing costs more than the payout it protects; this is where the 40% rule certainly applies per all sources read.
11. Compliance guardrails as constraints, not tunables: holds well over 5 seconds, no cross-account hedge, news blackout where required, flat by 4:45 pm ET.
